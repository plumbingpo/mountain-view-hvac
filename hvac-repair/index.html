When to Call for HVAC Repair in Mountain View

Recognizing the warning signs early can save you time, money, and prevent significant damage to your property. Keep an eye out for the following clues that it's time to bring in an expert:

  • Puddles near the furnace or air handler from a blocked drain line
  • The outdoor unit humming but refusing to actually start
  • The system short-cycling on and off without reaching the set temperature
  • Barely any air coming out of the vents
  • Higher energy bills with no improvement in comfort
  • Unfamiliar sounds from the equipment, like squealing, grinding, or banging
  • A circuit breaker that keeps tripping when the system starts
  • Air at the registers that is the wrong temperature for the mode you selected

The Benefits of Professional HVAC Repair

Start with safety. Outdoor units and air handlers are fed by 240-volt circuits, capacitors keep a painful charge long after the power is shut off, refrigerant cannot legally be handled without recovery equipment, and a gas furnace combines fuel piping, open combustion, and flue gases, where small errors lead to gas leaks or carbon monoxide exposure. Then add the diagnostic problem. A system that runs but does not cool could have a failing capacitor, a low charge, an iced-over evaporator, or simply poor airflow, and buying parts until the symptom disappears rarely ends cheaply.

Maintenance Tips for HVAC Repair

Smart maintenance is almost always cheaper and easier than an early replacement. Put these technician-approved steps to work around your property:

  • Do not ignore new sounds; catching a noisy motor before it locks up saves real money
  • Leave every supply and return vent open and free of furniture or rugs
  • Flush the condensate drain line each spring before algae can clog it and trip the float switch
  • Ease the strain in extreme weather by nudging the thermostat a few degrees toward the outdoor temperature
  • Keep the outdoor unit clear of grass, leaves, and shrubs so the condenser coil can breathe
  • Change the filter every one to three months so the blower and coil never starve for air
  • Book a professional tune-up in spring for cooling and in fall for heating
